Linear Algebra

Vectors, matrices as motions of space, determinants and eigenvectors.

The series treats a matrix as a motion of space. Vectors come first, then matrices as transformations and multiplication as doing one after another. The determinant, the dot product and eigenvectors each describe something a transformation does, and the last lesson solves systems of equations by running a transformation backward.

Start with lesson 1
  1. 1 What is a vector? Arrows, lists of numbers, and adding themA vector is a list of numbers read as a movement. Adding entry by entry matches placing arrows tip to tail, and two non-parallel arrows reach the whole plane.22 min
  2. 2 A matrix is a transformation of spaceA 2x2 matrix moves every point of the plane, and its two columns say where the basis arrows land. Linearity is why those four numbers settle everything else.18 min
  3. 3 Why matrix multiplication is defined the way it isMultiplying matrices means doing one transformation after another. The rows-times-columns rule, the failure of AB = BA and associativity all follow from that.20 min
  4. 4 The determinant is how much area gets scaledA matrix multiplies every area in the plane by one number, ad - bc. That explains its sign, why det(AB) = det(A)det(B), and which matrices can be undone.23 min
  5. 5 The dot product: projection, angle and why the formula worksMultiply matching coordinates and add. The result measures how far one arrow reaches along another, which gives angles, projections and Cauchy-Schwarz.23 min
  6. 6 Eigenvectors: the directions a transformation leaves aloneMost arrows are knocked off their line by a matrix. The few that are only stretched are its eigenvectors, and they turn matrix powers into powers of numbers.23 min
  7. 7 Solving Ax = b by undoing a transformationA linear system asks which vector a matrix sends to b. That view explains when there is one solution, none or a whole line, and what the inverse matrix does.19 min
